Permissions

Claude checks with you before it touches your files, tools or the browser. You decide how often it asks, and what to allow when it does.

Approvals

How often Claude checks in

Use the approval control under the message box:

  • Manually approve — Claude asks before every action. Use it for new tasks and anything with client files.
  • Automatically approve — Claude works on its own, blocks anything unsafe, and only stops when it needs you. Use it for routine work you trust, like a carrier submission you run all the time.

What each choice means

When Claude does ask, you get three choices:

ChoiceWhat happensGood for
Always allowIt stops asking for that kind of action.Carrier submissions; reading a folder you shared.
This time onlyIt asks again next time.Changing or overwriting a file; deleting; anything outside the task.
DenyIt doesn’t do it.Anything you don’t want — then tell it what to do instead.

When in doubt, pick This time only. In Chrome the same choice reads “All for this website” (like Always allow) — pick that for carrier portals so Claude isn’t stopped at every step.

Permissions control what Claude may do. What it can reach is separate — only files and folders you’ve shared with it.

🛡️ Working safely with client data

Because we handle client PII, a few habits keep it safe:

  • Use Manually approve for anything sensitive, and read Claude’s prompts before clicking — it always asks before permanently deleting a file.
  • Share a specific folder, never your whole drive, and close confidential files you don’t need open.
  • Only run on files you trust. An outside file or email can hide text that tries to redirect Claude. Claude is built to resist this and will ask you to confirm anything risky — read those confirmations. When unsure, work on a trusted copy.
  • Keep client dashboards to invited people — share them with Only people invited, not everyone at Labarre/Oksnee (see Sharing & comments).
  • Review before it’s final — you’re the expert.
⚠️ Don’t point Claude in Chrome at pages with client PIIIt takes a picture of your tab to see it, so avoid pages showing client personal info or sensitive account data. It’s fine for navigating a portal or filling a form — just mind what’s on screen.

HO-6 Flyer

/lo-ho-6-generator — a personal-lines recommendation flyer for condo & townhome unit owners.

What it does

Builds a one-page LaBarre Oksnee HO-6 flyer (Word .docx) that tells a condo or townhome unit owner what personal insurance to carry — worded to match the association’s master policy. Hand it to owners or the management company so their personal coverage lines up with what the master policy does and doesn’t cover.

How to run it

  1. Attach the association’s master policy proposal into Claude (the SB1525 disclosure, dec pages, or a coverage summary work too).
  2. Run /lo-ho-6-generator.
  3. Claude reads the property form off the policy language and outputs one finished flyer for that association — filled in and ready to send, not a blank template.
💡 It tailors the flyer to the proposalClaude reads the actual proposal and adapts the flyer to that association: it drops any coverage the association doesn’t carry (for example, no Workers’ Comp if there are no employees), matches the real deductible list, and picks the correct property form from the policy language instead of leaving that call to you.

The three master property forms

The master property form decides how much of the unit interior the master covers — and therefore what the owner still needs to insure. Claude reads which one applies from the policy language and builds the single matching flyer:

  • Single-entity — the most common condo form. The master covers the interior as originally built; the owner insures their betterments and upgrades.
  • Bare-walls — the master stops at the studs; the owner insures the entire interior build-out.
  • All-in — the master covers the interior plus upgrades; the owner mainly needs personal property and confirms valuations.
If the form is unclearWhen a proposal is genuinely ambiguous about the form, Claude defaults to single-entity (the most common condo form) and says so in its reply — along with the policy language it based that on — so you can double-check it before the flyer goes to owners.

No proposal? You get the blank templates

Without a proposal to read, Claude can’t know which form applies — so instead of one finished flyer it gives you three blank templates (one per form) to fill in yourself. Replace the placeholders:

  • [Association Name] and the policy term dates.
  • The deductibles — [$AOP], [$WATER], [$FIRE], and [EQ %] — each appears twice (in the bold/underlined paragraph and again in the third bullet), so replace both.

The blank templates list all five master coverages — General Liability, D&O, Fidelity/Crime, Workers’ Comp, and Umbrella — plus the four deductibles.
